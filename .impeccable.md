# Design context — rulers.ofthepast.org

A synchronic atlas of world rulers, from antiquity to the eighteenth century,
where you move a **meridian** to any year and see who held power across the world
at the same moment — and, for those whose lives are well enough recorded, speak
with an avatar of them.

Sibling in machinery to `rulers.ofancientrome.org` (ROAR) and the OHM map
atlases, but a **different material**. ROAR is an inscription **cut** in
travertine — one line of succession, the face and the catalogue mark. This is a
world chronicle **drawn and inked** on vellum — *the whole world on a spine of
time*. The hero is the **year**, and the revelation is **simultaneity**.

## Design Context

### Users
History-curious general readers, browsing out of curiosity (a museum-at-home,
not a research tool). The delight is the "I didn't realise they were alive at the
same time" moment: Charlemagne and Harun al-Rashid and Empress Irene and Emperor
Kanmu, all in the year 800. Often on a phone, unhurried.

### Brand personality
Three words: **cosmographic, simultaneous, unhurried.** The voice of an early-
modern world chronicle or star-atlas — learned, dignified, a little wondrous at
the breadth of the world. Dignity over decoration; no flags, no clip-art globes.

### Aesthetic direction — THE SYNCHRONIC CHRONICLE (committed)

The page is a **cosmographic almanac**: the world's thrones charted along a
moving line of time. Where Rome is carved stone, the Past is inked vellum.

- **Theme**: LIGHT = aged **vellum** (a daylit scriptorium), warm cream ground,
  iron-gall brown ink. DARK = the **astral night** (the same world under the
  night sky), deep indigo with parchment-gold ink. The toggle is day/night, not
  light/dark-mode-with-glow.
- **Type**: display **Cormorant Garamond** (high-contrast humanist serif of the
  printed chronicle); labels **Cormorant SC** (letter-spaced small caps); long
  reading **Spectral** (a scholarly screen serif). Deliberately *not* ROAR's
  Marcellus/Cardo — siblings, not twins.
- **Colour** (OKLCH, neutrals tinted warm): vellum ground, iron-gall ink,
  **gold-leaf** hairlines (the shared sibling thread with ROAR). A **rubric red**
  is the one interactive accent — the line of "now" on the meridian, the speak
  button. Ten **region inks** (low-chroma, one per lane) and four **era washes**
  (the meridian bands) carry meaning — they appear as a lane rule, a glyph dot, a
  medallion ring, a timeline band, **never as a fill or a border stripe**.
- **Texture of craft**: soft 2px radius (drawn, not cut), **recessed** insets and
  hairline rules, circular **roundel** portraits (coins / chart medallions), wide
  quiet margins.

### Signature moves (what you remember)
- **The Meridian** — a big year readout over a time-rule banded by era; its
  width-per-era is proportional to how many rulers it holds, so the dense ages
  get room and the deep past stays compact. Drag it, key it, or **let time
  drift** (a constant-speed sweep) and the world re-lights.
- **Region lanes** — the world laid out as horizontal lanes, each with a
  small-caps name and an ink-coloured glyph; the rulers reigning *this year*
  appear as portrait **medallions** with name, realm, and reign.
- **The reading** — a single italic line: *"N rulers held power across M regions
  of the world."*
- **Reigning elsewhere at the same time** — every detail folio surfaces the
  contemporaries, one per region, turning a single life into a snapshot of the
  whole world's thrones.
- The far edge hands off **back to Rome** (`rulers.ofancientrome.org`) — closing
  the sibling loop the other way.

### Anti-references / banned
Not a SaaS dashboard, not a rounded-card grid with soft drop shadows, not dark-
mode-with-glowing-accents, not a literal world map with pins. Explicitly avoided:
side-stripe borders, gradient text, glassmorphism, neon region colours, reflex
fonts (Cinzel/EB Garamond).

### Design principles
1. **Time is the hero.** The year leads; everything answers "who, at the same
   moment, elsewhere?"
2. **Ink, don't cut.** Depth from recesses and hairlines; the round of a medallion
   is the signature shape.
3. **Pigment is rare and meaningful.** Region inks and era washes earn their place;
   gold is a hairline, rubric red is the single accent.
4. **The face leads.** Portraits are the content; chrome stays quiet.
5. **Honest sparseness.** Some years are thin — that is a truth about the record,
   not a bug. Empty lanes say so plainly.

## House rules

- No build step. Plain HTML/CSS/JS, MapLibre-free. Relative paths only.
- `data/` is machine-owned (harvester output). Never hand-edit it; the roster
  lives in `harvester/build_seed.py`.
- Accessibility: the meridian rule is a real ARIA slider (arrow keys, Home/End),
  focus states everywhere, keyboard-closable drawer/chat, alt text on portraits,
  `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured (the sweep and the medallion rise-in stop).
- Portraits/biographies hot-linked from Wikimedia/Wikipedia; keep the credit in
  the detail folio and footer. Footer is always
  "Made with <3 in Bologna by OpenHistoryMap".
